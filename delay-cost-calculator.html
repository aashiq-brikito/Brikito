How the delay cost is calculated

A delayed site keeps spending even when work stops. The calculator adds up the costs that run every calendar day of delay.

CostHow it is worked out
Site overheadsThe daily figure you enter: staff, site office, security, utilities and insurance.
Idle labourWorkers left idle × average daily wage.
Idle equipmentDaily hire you keep paying for machines standing idle.
Finance costLoan or capital deployed × interest rate ÷ 365.
Deferred revenueRent, lease or sales receipts pushed back each day (optional).
Liquidated damagesProject value × LD % per week ÷ 7, if your contract has an LD clause (optional, uncapped).

This is an estimate to help you size the problem. It does not include escalation in material prices, extended supervision fees or claims, which usually make a real delay cost more.

Three ways to stop paying for delays

See slippage on day one

Compare planned vs actual every day, not at the monthly review. A 2-day slip is cheap to recover; a 3-week slip is not.

Turn hindrances into actions

Every blocker needs an owner and a date: a drawing awaited, a delivery late, an inspection pending. Chase the owner, not the site.

Stop idle labour and machines

Know tomorrow's inputs today. If cement or a drawing will not arrive, move the crew and the crane before they stand idle.

Frequently asked questions

How do you calculate the cost of construction delay per day?

Add the costs that keep running every day of delay: site overheads, idle labour (workers × daily wage), idle equipment hire, interest on the capital deployed (amount × annual rate ÷ 365), any rent or revenue deferred, and liquidated damages if your contract has them.

What is a typical cost of delay on an Indian building project?

It depends on project size and financing. For a ₹50 crore residential project with ₹20 crore deployed at 11%, about 40 idle workers and ₹35,000 of daily hire, the daily cost comes to roughly ₹1.8 lakh, before any liquidated damages.

What are liquidated damages (LD) in construction?

Liquidated damages are a pre-agreed amount a contractor pays the owner for each week of delay beyond the contract completion date, often 0.5% of contract value per week, capped at 5–10% of the contract value.

Is my data stored?

Only if you ask for the report: we save your inputs and contact details to send the PDF and follow up. The calculator itself runs in your browser.
